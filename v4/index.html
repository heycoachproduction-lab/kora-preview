How to judge what she is worth

Saving you hours is the small half.
The other half is money.

Most of what a business loses is not wasted spending. It is money already earned that quietly leaves, and money sitting inside the company that nobody went and collected. KORA is built to count those separately — and to show her working.

How KORA counts. Three numbers, each traced to a real event.

Example figures · not a client result
Generated $6,800

  • A lead answered in minutes instead of days
  • Past clients who were due the same service again
  • A second service the client already needed
Recovered $4,200

Money already earned that was on its way out of the door.

  • An invoice past due, chased and paid
  • A contract about to lapse, renewed in time
  • A quote nobody had answered, reopened
Saved $1,700

Money the business did not spend that it was about to.

  • Two overlapping tools, one cancelled
  • Work drafted here instead of bought outside
  • An auto‑renew stopped before it billed

Each line traces back to one event, with a date and a source you can open.

Three numbers, never one. KORA will not blend them into a single flattering ROI figure. Money saved is not the same thing as money earned, and an owner deserves to see which is which.

Only what she can prove. No estimated hours, no assumed value, no “probably would have”. If KORA cannot show you the event behind a number, the number does not get counted — and she will tell you that too.

Straight answers

The six things owners actually ask.

Is this just a chatbot with a box around it?

No. A chatbot answers a question and forgets you. KORA keeps the approved context of one specific business — what was decided, what is happening now, and what she is allowed to do — and carries operating work forward inside those limits.

Talking is how you reach her. It is not what she is.

What exactly am I paying $300 a month for?

For KORA working, not for permission to use software. It covers her configuration for how your business actually runs, keeping her current, the routing that decides what runs on your own Core and what does not, new rules and workflows as you ask for them, and a team behind her when something needs a person.

Paid yearly it is $2,400 — $200 a month. Month to month it is $300, and you can stop whenever you like.

Where does my business data actually live?

The Core sits in your business, and the ordinary day‑to‑day work happens on it. We do not claim that nothing ever leaves — that would not be true of any capable system, and we would rather be straight with you.

When a task genuinely needs the strongest outside model, KORA uses one, and you set the rules for what may go out and what may not. Access is not authority: being able to see something is not the same as being allowed to act on it.

What happens if I stop paying?

The Core stays yours. Your data stays yours. Everything KORA already built for the business stays. It was bought, not licensed.

What stops is the service around her: configuration, routing, updates, hosted infrastructure and support.

When does the subscription actually start?

After your Core is delivered and activated — not when it ships. Days spent in a courier van are never days you paid for.

What if we grow and outgrow the one we bought?

You upgrade the Core, you do not replace it. Memory and storage go up inside the same machine. Growing a business should not mean buying the whole thing again.

People are added separately: $600 a year each, or $99 a month.
